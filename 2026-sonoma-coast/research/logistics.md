# Bodega Bay & Russian River, Thanksgiving 2026: routes, logistics, safety and prep

Research date: **Tue 2026-11-10 → Wed 2026-11-11** (Pacific time). Trip: **Thu 11/26 → Sat 11/28/2026** (2 nights). 3 adults (maybe 4), Korean students at UC Berkeley (some may be international students). Own regular 2WD car, Berkeley → Bodega Bay / Sonoma Coast / Russian River (Jenner, Guerneville, Occidental, Sebastopol), optionally via Point Reyes.

## TL;DR

- **Fastest route (≈ 68 mi, ~1 h 35 min free-flow):** Berkeley → I-80/I-580 W → **Richmond–San Rafael (R-SR) Bridge** → US-101 N → Petaluma/Cotati → Valley Ford Rd → CA-1 → Bodega Bay [Data, OSRM]. **Scenic route (≈ 72 mi, ~2 h 10 min free-flow, without stops):** R-SR Bridge → Sir Francis Drake Blvd → Point Reyes Station → CA-1 along Tomales Bay → Bodega Bay. Adding the Point Reyes Lighthouse makes the Thursday drive **≈ 110 mi / ~3 h 25 min of driving**. Best return: CA-116 along the river (Guerneville → Forestville → Sebastopol) → US-101 S → R-SR Bridge (≈ 70 mi, ~1 h 40 min).
- **Tolls:** the only toll on the standard loop is the **R-SR Bridge westbound, $8.50** (since Jan 1, 2026; same for FasTrak and non-FasTrak). Eastbound coming home is **free**. All tolling is electronic, with no cash booths. Without FasTrak, a **toll invoice is mailed to the registered owner**; you can also pay through a **Short-Term License Plate Account**, which covers crossings in the past 48 h or up to 30 days ahead [Official, 511/BATA]. The **Golden Gate Bridge** is tolled only **southbound** (FasTrak $10.25 / one-time payment $10.50 / invoice $11.25 since 7/1/2026), so you'd pay it only if you come home through San Francisco [Official, GGBHTD].
- **Thanksgiving traffic:** AAA/INRIX 2025 said "**minimal traffic impact**" on Thanksgiving Day. Saturday was worst **1–8 pm** and best **before 10 am**. The Bay Area's worst 2025 corridor was **SF → Santa Rosa on US-101 N, Tuesday 7:15 pm (2 h 34 m, +114 %)**, which is our road but not our day [Official, AAA 11/17/2025]. The 2026 forecast isn't out yet; AAA usually publishes it the week before. **Plan: drive out Thu morning, drive home Sat before ~1 pm.**
- **Thanksgiving-day hours that change the route:** the Point Reyes **Lighthouse Visitor Center and lighthouse stairs are open 10 am–2 pm only on Thu 11/26** (10–4 on Fri/Sat). The **Bear Valley Visitor Center is open 9:30 am–2 pm on Thu** (9:30–5 other days). The stairs close when winds are above 40 mph [Official, NPS API]. If you do the lighthouse on Thursday, be at the lot by about **12:30**.
- **Daylight is short.** Sunset is **4:53 pm** at Bodega Bay all three days, and civil twilight ends at **5:22 pm**. Sunrise is 7:05–7:07 am [Official, USNO]. Plan to be off CA-1's cliff stretches and the Bohemian Hwy before dark.
- **Weather (1991–2020 normals, 11/26–28):** coast (Bodega Head) highs are **~58 °F** and lows **~45 °F**. Inland near the river (Graton/Santa Rosa) it's **~61–62 °F / ~38 °F**. The chance of measurable rain is **~32–37 % per day** [Official, NCEI]. The NOAA CPC week 3–4 outlook (issued 11/6, valid 11/21–12/4) **favors above-normal precipitation for the West Coast, highest across much of California (El Niño)**, plus above-normal temperatures [Official, CPC].
- **Big ocean signal right now.** NWS Bay Area (11/10) expects **20–30 ft surf** on 11/13–14. Wright's Beach Campground is closed 11/13–17 for the swell. NWS also flagged that **king tides in Thanksgiving week will be ~0.8 ft higher** than this week's, and could approach a 1-in-100-year total water level if they coincide with SW winds or low pressure. That is "something to keep an eye on for the week of Thanksgiving" [Official, NWS AFD 11/10]. The Coastal Commission's statewide **King Tides are Nov 24–26, 2026** [Official].
- **Tides on our dates:** midday highs are near king-tide level (**~6.0–6.8 ft at Bodega Harbor entrance, ~11 am–1 pm**). The good minus low tides come **after dark** (6:26 pm Thu, 7:19 pm Fri, 8:14 pm Sat) [Official, NOAA CO-OPS]. That means no real daytime tidepooling, and the beaches are narrowest and most dangerous around midday.
- **Ocean safety is the #1 hazard here.** The Sonoma Coast is "**NOT FOR SWIMMING**". Duncan's Landing is "the most dangerous point along the Sonoma Coast". The **Goat Rock gate is closed to cars when ocean conditions are dangerous**. NPS says sneaker waves "are unpredictable" and advises staying **at least 30 yards from the water** on open-ocean beaches. Never turn your back on the ocean, and stay off rocks and logs [Official, State Parks, NPS].
- **Road status (Caltrans 11/11):** no restrictions on SR-1 in Sonoma or Marin, or on US-101 or SR-37 in the Bay Area. **CA-116 has two one-way traffic controls in Monte Rio**: slide repair (Duncan Rd–Monte Cristo Ave, 24/7 through **4/30/27**, ~5 min delay) and a retaining wall at Cnopius Rd (24/7 through **11/30/26**, ~10 min delay) [Official, Caltrans].
- **Russian River flood risk is low but real.** The Guerneville gauge read **4.3 ft on 11/11; flood stage is 32 ft**. NWS lists CA-116 closing **west of Duncan Mills at 39 ft** and **west of Guerneville at 44 ft**. The last big floods were **45.4 ft in Feb 2019** and 33.4 ft in Dec 2014 [Official, NWPS]. Floods need days of heavy rain, so only worry if an atmospheric river is forecast.
- **Gas** (AAA, 11/10/26): CA average **$5.71** (a year ago $4.72). Santa Rosa $5.81, San Rafael $5.73, Oakland $5.70. The loop is about **230–250 mi**, so about **$45–60 per car (~$15–20 each with 3 people)**. Fill up in Berkeley, Petaluma, Sebastopol or Point Reyes Station. NPS: the "closest gas station [to the lighthouse] is 20 miles away in Point Reyes Station".
- **Parking/fees:**
  - **Point Reyes**: free.
  - **Sonoma Coast State Park**: free at most beach lots; **$10 only at campground lots** (Bodega Dunes, Wright's Beach).
  - **Armstrong Redwoods**: **$10** per car, or park on Armstrong Woods Rd and walk in.
  - **Doran Regional Park**: **$8**.
  - **Samuel P. Taylor SP and Tomales Bay SP**: **$8**.
  - A free **California State Library Parks Pass** (borrowed with a public-library card) covers vehicle day use at Sonoma Coast SP and Armstrong Redwoods [Official].
- **Cell coverage:** NPS says it is "very minimal to nonexistent" at the Point Reyes Lighthouse, and expect gaps on CA-1 and in the river canyon (**UNVERIFIED** detail). **Download offline maps.** The nearest **ERs** are in **Santa Rosa** (≈ 30–55 min from the coast) and **Petaluma**; for Point Reyes, Petaluma/Novato/Greenbrae. **The Sebastopol hospital is now a long-term acute care hospital, not an ER.**

**On the standard loop you pay exactly one toll: $8.50 on the R-SR Bridge on the way out.** Coming home over the R-SR Bridge eastbound is free. Coming home over the Golden Gate costs $10.25–11.25, and the Bay Bridge eastbound after it is free.

### 4.2 Paying without FasTrak
- All Bay Area bridges are electronic with no cash booths. "If you cross a toll bridge without a FasTrak toll tag or License Plate Account, a toll invoice will be mailed to the vehicle's registered owner" [Official, 511].
- **Short-Term License Plate Account** (bayareafastrak.org): "Pay tolls from the past 48 hours or for travel up to 30 days in the future." It can't be used for express lanes or carpool discounts [Official, 511]. **Use it if the car is registered to someone else** (parents, an out-of-state address, a DMV address that isn't current), so the invoice doesn't go astray.
- Golden Gate invoices: the first is mailed ~1 week after crossing (California plates). "This Toll Invoice must be paid within 21 days". Unpaid invoices escalate to a violation with a **$25 penalty per toll**, then DMV registration holds; out-of-state plates go to collections [Official, GGBHTD].
- If the car already has a **FasTrak tag**, it is charged automatically. Make sure the tag is mounted and the account has a balance.
- Help: Bay Area FasTrak **1-877-229-8655**.

## 8. Driving the curvy roads, at night, in fog

- **Roads:**
  - **CA-1 Point Reyes Station → Bodega Bay** (32 mi): narrow and curvy, right beside Tomales Bay at Marshall.
  - **CA-1 Bodega Bay → Jenner** (10.6 mi): bluffs with steep drop-offs in places, no street lights.
  - **CA-116 Jenner → Guerneville**: a river road with the Monte Rio one-way controls.
  - **Bohemian Hwy**: very narrow and twisty.
  - **Sir Francis Drake to the lighthouse**: NPS calls it "windy and slow-going", passing working ranches, with cattle and ranch vehicles possible.
- **Turnout law:** on a two-lane road where passing is unsafe, a slow vehicle with **5 or more cars** behind it must pull over at the next turnout (California Vehicle Code §21656). Use the pullouts, and let locals pass.
- **Fog** (Caltrans tips): slow down, use **low beams** (never only parking or fog lights, never high beams), and use the right-hand "fog line" as a guide. "Nearly three in four fog-related traffic collisions are connected to motorists driving too fast" [Official, Caltrans]. Morning fog filled the Sonoma valleys on 11/10–11 [Official, NWS AFD].
- **After dark:** sunset is 4:53 pm. Plan the last cliff/river drive of each day to end by ~5:15 pm. Deer are common at dusk on all these roads (**UNVERIFIED**/general). There are no sidewalks on CA-1/CA-116 in most places, so if walking to dinner, carry a light and wear something bright.
- **Rain:** the first rains make roads slick (oil). Wet bluff trails and rocks are slippery. NPS warns that "Heavy rains … can lead to seasonal trail flooding and washouts".
- **Carsickness:** Bohemian Hwy and CA-1 are long sequences of curves. Have the prone person sit in front, and take medication *before* the drive.
- **Phone use:** hands-free only while driving (California law). The navigator does the maps.

### 9.3 Storm impacts to plan around
- **High surf + king tides (Nov 24–26) + a storm** is the worst-case combination for this trip: closed beach access (Goat Rock gate), waves over parking lots at Duncan's Landing, and minor flooding of low spots next to the water (CA-1 along Tomales Bay; Doran Beach Rd; Bodega Harbor). Road-specific king-tide flooding on our route is **UNVERIFIED**; watch for **Coastal Flood Advisories** from NWS. SR-37 (route E) is the classic Bay Area king-tide road, which is another reason to skip it.
- **Russian River flooding** (NWS/NWPS, Russian River at Guerneville, GUEC1) [Official]:
  - Stage **4.31 ft on 11/11**, forecast ~4.6 ft by 11/15 (no flooding).
  - Thresholds: **action 29 ft, minor (flood stage) 32 ft, moderate 35 ft, major 40 ft**.
  - Road impacts listed by NWS:
    - 32 ft: lower Mill St (Guerneville) and Old Bohemian Hwy (Monte Rio) flood.
    - 33 ft: CA-116 at Drake Rd closed.
    - **39 ft: "West of Duncan Mills HWY 116 is closed."**
    - 41.5 ft: River Road closed in Guerneville.
    - **44 ft: "In Guerneville HWY 116 west of town closed. Safeway floods."**
    - 45–46 ft: "Disastrous flooding … Many roads will flood making travel nearly impossible."
  - Recent crests: **45.38 ft (2/27/2019)**, 37.82 ft (1/2017), 33.42 ft (12/12/2014), 31.83 ft (1/2023). An early-season storm in **Oct 2021 reached only 23.49 ft**. Record: 49.5 ft (2/1986). Most floods happen **Dec–Mar** after the soil is saturated.
  - **Bottom line:** a late-November flood would need several days of heavy atmospheric-river rain beforehand. If NWS issues a **Flood Watch for the Russian River**, or the CNRFC forecast for Guerneville approaches ~29 ft, **re-plan** (stay on the coast or near US-101), because CA-116 and River Rd are the lifelines into Guerneville.
- **Slides and downed trees:** both CA-116 one-way controls are already slide or retaining-wall repairs. On CA-1 south of Point Reyes, the Muir Beach–Stinson stretch needed a 4-month, $25 M rebuild after a 2007 slide [Secondary, Wikipedia]. In heavy rain, check QuickMap before each drive.
- **Wind:** the Point Reyes headlands are among the windiest spots on the coast. The lighthouse stairs close above 40 mph [Official, NPS].

## 12. Ocean, bluff and wildlife safety

**Ocean** [Official, State Parks; NPS]:
- "Like most north coast beaches, Sonoma Coast is **NOT FOR SWIMMING**. Strong rip currents, heavy surf and sudden ground swells make surf play dangerous." Lifeguards are limited or absent. Point Reyes has **no lifeguards**.
- **Sneaker waves:** "unexpectedly large wave[s] … reaching farther up the beach to levels far beyond where the normal waves reach." They "can occur at any time, day or night … during storms and during sunny calm weather". NPS warns they are often preceded by a sudden lowering of the water level, and that they "toss around large driftwood logs".
  - **Stay at least 30 yards from the water** on open-ocean beaches.
  - **Never turn your back on the surf.**
  - Don't stand on logs or wet rocks.
  - Two children died at Point Reyes' South Beach from sneaker waves in 2001, and a visitor was knocked off a rock at McClures Beach in 2004 (NPS).
- "Most victims were climbing on rocks and cliffs, playing in or near the surf, or shore fishing" (State Parks brochure).
- **If someone is swept in:** call **911**, keep them in sight, give your location, and "**Do not attempt a water rescue yourself**" (State Parks). For rip currents: don't fight it, swim parallel to shore, then angle in (NPS).
- **Cold water:** NPS says "Do not stay in the water for more than a few minutes unless you are wearing a wetsuit."
- **Check before each beach day:** NWS Bay Area "Watches, Warnings & Advisories" for the **coastal North Bay** (Beach Hazards Statement / High Surf Advisory / High Surf Warning), as NPS recommends. If a High Surf Warning is up, enjoy the waves **from high viewpoints** (Bodega Head bluffs, Goat Rock overlook, the lighthouse), not the sand.

**Bluffs:** stay back from cliff edges and away from cliff bases (rockfall). Don't climb the shale. Collapses include part of the Arch Rock overlook (2015) and Tomales Point bluff (2017) [Official, NPS].

**Tsunami:** the beaches and low parts of Bodega Bay are tsunami zones. If you feel strong or long shaking near the coast, go to high ground immediately without waiting for an official alert [Official, NPS beach safety page links to NOAA/USGS tsunami info].

**Wildlife and plants:**
- Harbor seals at Goat Rock: stay **≥ 50 yards** away (State Parks).
- Tule elk (Tomales Point) are wild and large; keep well back.
- Ticks (Lyme disease; NPS: ~1 % of adult ticks / 3 % of nymphs locally infected), so do tick checks after grassy trails. Learn to recognize poison oak (leaves of three). Mountain lions are "very rare" (NPS).
- **Shellfish:** CDPH runs an annual mussel quarantine (May–Oct) and has issued Marin PSP advisories. Don't eat self-harvested shellfish without checking CDPH [Official, NPS conditions page].
- Recreational Dungeness crab season opened **Sat 11/7/2026**, with a temporary restriction on standard crab traps (hoop nets/snares OK) [Official, Sonoma County Regional Parks].

## 13. Car break-ins and personal safety

- Beach and trailhead lots are remote and often empty in the off-season. The lighthouse requires leaving the car for 1–2 h, and Bodega Head and Goat Rock lots have no attendants. No official Sonoma/Marin break-in statistics were found (**UNVERIFIED** frequency), but smash-and-grabs at Bay Area scenic lots are a known pattern, and the precautions are free:
  - **Put bags in the trunk before you arrive** (not in the lot, where someone can watch).
  - Leave **nothing visible**: no phone mounts or cables, no shopping bags, no jackets on seats.
  - Carry passports, laptops and wallets with you, or leave them at the lodging. **Unload at the lodging before sightseeing on the last day**; don't leave luggage in the car at the Sat stops.
  - Park in the open, near other cars.
- Thanksgiving is a high-risk period for impaired driving (AAA/MADD numbers in §3.1). The driver doesn't drink; if dinner involves wine, pick a place you can walk to or take turns driving.
- The towns (Bodega Bay, Guerneville, Sebastopol, Occidental) are small and generally calm. The main personal risks are traffic, the ocean and the dark, not crime.
