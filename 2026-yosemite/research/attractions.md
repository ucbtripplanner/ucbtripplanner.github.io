# Yosemite: Sights, Hikes & First-Timer Itineraries (Thanksgiving weekend 2026)

**Researched:** 2026-11-07 · **Trip:** 2 nights / 3 days within Thu Nov 26 – Sun Nov 29, 2026 · 3 first-time visitors · own car from Berkeley

**How to read this:** Facts come with a source link. Anything I could not confirm for 2026 is marked **UNVERIFIED**, along with the most recent information I found and its date. Advice I worked out myself (photo light, timing guesses) is labeled *(analysis)*.

---

## 0. TL;DR

- **No reservation or timed entry is needed in 2026.** The park dropped the reservation system for all of 2026 ([NPS, updated 2026-02-18](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/reservations.htm)).
- **Fees:** $35 per car, good for 7 days, card only (no cash). On top of that, **every non-US resident aged 16+ pays $100**, unless someone in the car has an annual or America the Beautiful pass ([NPS fees](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/fees.htm)). If all 3 travelers count as non-residents, buy the **$250 Non-Resident America the Beautiful pass**. It covers the whole car, so you pay $250 instead of $35 + 3×$100 = $335. Residency is explained in §4.
- **The core first-timer course, all open year-round in Yosemite Valley:**
  - Tunnel View
  - Bridalveil Fall
  - Lower Yosemite Fall
  - Cook's Meadow and Sentinel Bridge
  - Mist Trail to the Vernal Fall footbridge, or to the top of Vernal Fall
  - El Capitan Meadow and Valley View
- **Second tier:** Mirror Lake, Columbia Rock, and a giant sequoia grove (Mariposa Grove, or Tuolumne Grove if you drive in on Hwy 120).
- **Late-November caveats:**
  - **Glacier Point Road and Tioga Road were still open on 2026-11-06, but they normally close for the winter in mid-November**, and in recent years have rarely been open past Nov 26. Don't build the plan around Glacier Point.
  - Waterfalls are "flowing low" ([NPS conditions, 2026-11-06](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)).
  - Trails are icy in shaded spots.
  - **You must carry tire chains** whenever chain controls are posted, even in an AWD car or a rental ([NPS tire chains](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/tirechains.htm)).
- **Daylight:** sunrise about 6:51 AM, sunset about 4:41 PM. That is under 10 hours, and hikes must finish by about 4 PM.

### 1.2 Trails with seasonal closures ([seasonal dates dataset](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/seasonal.htm))

- **Mist Trail.** The lower section along Vernal Fall closes in winter because of falling ice and rock ([NPS Vernal/Nevada page, updated 2026-11-02](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/vernalnevadatrail.htm)).
  - Recent closing dates: 2024 Dec 20; 2023 Jan 7, 2024; 2022 Nov 11; 2021 Dec 10; 2020 Nov 11 (reopened late Nov); 2019 Nov 26; 2018 Dec 3; 2016 Nov 25; 2015 Nov 16.
  - **It was not listed as closed on 2026-11-06.** Its status on Nov 26–29 is **UNVERIFIED**; by history it is about a coin flip toward open.
  - When it is closed, the **winter route** to the top of Vernal Fall runs up the John Muir Trail to Clark Point, then down the left fork to the Mist Trail junction above the fall ([NPS winter route](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/vernalnevadawinter.htm)).
  - The Vernal Fall footbridge (0.8 mi up) is reachable either way.
- **Four Mile Trail.** "Closes below Union Point... after significant snow accumulation (usually by November or December)." When it is partly closed, only the lower 3 miles are open ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/fourmiletrail.htm)).
- **Half Dome:** the cables are down for the season ([conditions](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)). Not an option.
- **Valley Loop Trail:** the crossing over El Capitan Bridge is closed on weekdays through December for bridge work ([conditions](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)).
- **Dome Fire (Sept–Oct 2026):** 86% contained on Oct 13. "All south and eastbound trails accessed from Glacier Point Road" were closed ([NPS Dome Fire update 2026-10-13](https://www.nps.gov/yose/learn/news/dome-fire-update-october-13-2026-morning.htm)). The Nov 6 conditions page still lists an area east of Wawona Rd and south of Glacier Point Rd as closed. None of this affects the Valley course.

### 1.3 Waterfalls

- **Nov 6, 2026:** "Yosemite Falls, Vernal Fall, Nevada Fall, and Bridalveil Fall are flowing low, as is usual for this time of year" ([conditions](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)).
- **Yosemite Falls** "flows approximately November through July" and "storms in late fall rejuvenate some of the waterfalls" ([NPS waterfalls](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/waterfalls.htm)). In fall it may hold "little or no water" ([NPS fall](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/fall.htm)).
- **Vernal, Nevada and Bridalveil** run all year, but "slow to a trickle by fall."
- **Set expectations:** come for the granite walls, not big waterfalls. A rainstorm the week before could improve flow.

### 1.5 Chains / driving rules

- **"If you are visiting... from November through March, you should expect chain requirements to be in effect."**
- **Under any chain control, every vehicle must carry chains**, "including four-wheel drive and rental vehicles."
- Chains or cables must fit your tire size. Tire socks are legal; plastic and strap-type devices are not.
- **Chain levels:**
  - **R-1:** chains required unless the car is under 6,000 lb and has snow-rated (M+S) tires with at least 6/32" tread on the drive wheels.
  - **R-2:** chains required unless the car has 4WD/AWD engaged and M+S tires on all four wheels.
- Chains are required "more often" on Hwy 41, Hwy 120 (Big Oak Flat Rd) and Badger Pass Rd than on **Hwy 140 (El Portal Rd)**, the Valley roads, or Hetch Hetchy Rd.
- **Road info:** inside the park 209/372-0200 (1, 1); California highways 800/427-7623.
- Source for all of the above: [NPS Tire Chain Requirements](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/tirechains.htm).
- **Gas:** available 24 hours by credit card at Wawona, Crane Flat and El Portal. There is **no gas in Yosemite Valley** ([NPS driving](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/driving.htm)).

### 1.6 Crowds & parking

- **Valley parking:** "If you are planning to visit Yosemite Valley by car for the day, arrive before 9 am, after which parking is usually full" ([NPS Yosemite Valley](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yv.htm)).
- On "weekends and holidays during spring and fall," the target is before 8 AM. "Saturdays and holiday weekends [are] particularly busy." When the Valley lots are full, cars are turned back near El Capitan. "If you find a parking space, plan to leave your car there" ([NPS traffic](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/traffic.htm)).
- **Valley shuttle (free):** the Valleywide route runs 7 AM–10 PM every 12–22 minutes; the East Valley route every 8–12 minutes ([NPS transportation](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/publictransportation.htm)). It does **not** go to Bridalveil Fall or Tunnel View.
- **Traffic text alerts:** text `ynptraffic` to 333111 ([NPS traffic](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/traffic.htm)).
- **Holiday crowd levels for Thanksgiving 2026 are UNVERIFIED.** NPS calls late December and early January the busiest winter holiday period ([winter](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/winter.htm)). *(analysis)* Still, plan Fri and Sat as holiday-busy days: arrive early and park once.

## 2. Candidate sights, viewpoints and hikes (late-November-realistic)

**Priority labels:**
- **MUST:** core first-timer experience, low effort, works in late November.
- **RECOMMENDED:** worth it if time and conditions allow.
- **OPTIONAL:** only with extra time, special interest, or lucky road status.

### 2.1 Yosemite Valley — viewpoints

**1. Tunnel View — MUST**
- **Area / type:** Wawona Rd (Hwy 41), at the east end of the Wawona Tunnel, just above the Valley. Drive-up viewpoint.
- **Time:** 15–30 min.
- **Why it's a classic:** "One of the most famous views of Yosemite Valley." You see El Capitan, Bridalveil Fall and Half Dome together ([NPS Yosemite Valley](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yv.htm)).
- **Late-Nov status:** open year-round (Hwy 41 is maintained all winter). It can be icy.
- **Parking:** roadside lot at the tunnel's east portal. *(analysis)* It fills at sunset on busy days, so arrive 20–30 min early.
- **Best light** *(analysis)*: you look east up the Valley. Late afternoon to sunset puts the sun behind you, lighting El Capitan with alpenglow on Half Dome. At sunrise you face the sun, but valley fog can be beautiful. After a storm clears it is spectacular.
- **Getting there:** about 10 min to the Bridalveil parking from the Valley ([NPS driving times](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/driving.htm)). Tunnel View is a few minutes farther up Hwy 41.

**2. Valley View (Gates of the Valley) — MUST**
- **Area / type:** western Valley, on one-way Northside Drive, just before Pohono Bridge. Pull-out viewpoint.
- **Time:** 10–20 min.
- **Why it's a classic:** you "look up Yosemite Valley from alongside the Merced River." NPS says it is best to stop here on your way out of the Valley ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yv.htm)).
- **Late-Nov status:** open.
- **Parking:** small roadside pull-out.
- **Best light** *(analysis)*: late afternoon to sunset, or a calm morning for reflections in the river. Makes a natural farewell stop on departure day.

**3. El Capitan Meadow — MUST**
- **Area / type:** Valley, Northside Drive. Drive-up meadow viewpoint.
- **Time:** 10–20 min.
- **Why it's a classic:** "A view straight up El Capitan and a great view of Cathedral Rocks." Best stopped at on the way out ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yv.htm)).
- **Late-Nov status:** open, but **El Capitan Bridge** nearby has 15-min weekday delays and the Valley Loop Trail crossing there is closed on weekdays through Dec 2026 ([conditions](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)).
- **Parking:** roadside turnouts. Do not create new roadside parking.
- **Best light** *(analysis)*: late afternoon light on El Capitan's face. After dark, look for climbers' headlamps on the wall.

**4. Sentinel Bridge (Half Dome reflection) — MUST**
- **Area / type:** central Valley, near Yosemite Village. Short walk.
- **Time:** 10–20 min, or combine with Cook's Meadow.
- **Why it's a classic:** "Famous for its views of both Half Dome reflected in the Merced River," with Yosemite Falls nearby ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yv.htm)).
- **Late-Nov status:** open.
- **Parking:** "minimal parking" at Sentinel Bridge ([Cook's Meadow page](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/cooksmeadowtrail.htm)). Better to walk over from Yosemite Village.
- **Best light** *(analysis)*: sunset and alpenglow on Half Dome; calm water gives the best reflection.

**5. Glacier Point — MUST *if the road is open*, otherwise skip**
- **Area / type:** Glacier Point Rd, about 1 hr / 30 mi from the Valley. A 300-yd paved walk to the overlook, 3,200 ft above the Valley floor ([NPS Glacier Point hikes](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/glacierhikes.htm), [driving](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/driving.htm)).
- **Time:** 2.5–3 hr round trip from the Valley.
- **Why it's a classic:** "Iconic views of the valley floor, Half Dome, and the high Sierra."
- **Late-Nov status:** **probably closed for the season** (see §1.1). There is no overnight parking on the road. The Glacier Point tour bus only ran May 9 – Oct 12, 2026 ([Yosemite Hospitality tours](https://www.travelyosemite.com/things-to-do/guided-bus-tours)).
- **Best light** *(analysis)*: sunset on Half Dome, but you then drive back about an hour in the dark on a mountain road.

**6. Swinging Bridge / Yosemite Chapel / Sentinel Meadow area — OPTIONAL (quick photo stops)**
- **Why:** NPS names the "area around Sentinel Meadow, the Yosemite Chapel, and Yosemite Valley Lodge" as favorite places to see Yosemite Falls ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yv.htm)). The Swinging Bridge Picnic Area has vault toilets ([Four Mile page](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/fourmiletrail.htm)).
- **Time:** 10–15 min each.

### 2.2 Yosemite Valley — short walks

**7. Lower Yosemite Fall Loop — MUST**
- **Area / type:** Valley, shuttle stop #6. Easy paved loop.
- **Distance / effort:** 1 mi loop, about 50 ft gain, 30 min.
- **Why it's a classic:** "Views of both Upper and Lower Yosemite Falls." Yosemite Falls is "one of the world's tallest" waterfalls ([NPS trail](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/lowerfalltrail.htm), [waterfalls](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/waterfalls.htm)).
- **Late-Nov status:** open all year. Flow is low in late Nov. "Expect icy conditions... especially on the short climb leading to the footbridge on the western trail."
- **Tips:** walk it clockwise for the best views. Park at the Yosemite Village or Yosemite Falls lots, or take the shuttle. Restrooms and water are on the western trail.
- **Best light** *(analysis)*: morning to midday.

**8. Cook's Meadow Loop (+ Sentinel Bridge) — MUST**
- **Area / type:** Valley, starts at the visitor center (shuttle #5) or stop #6. Easy, flat, wheelchair-accessible when snow-free.
- **Distance / time:** 1 mi loop, 30 min.
- **Why it's a classic:** views of "Yosemite Falls, Half Dome, Glacier Point, and Sentinel Rock" from the heart of the Valley. The loop passes Sentinel Bridge ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/cooksmeadowtrail.htm)).
- **Late-Nov status:** open all year. "Boardwalks are slippery when wet and even more so when covered in snow or frost."
- **Best light:** NPS's own photo is a "morning view of Yosemite Falls from Cook's Meadow." Sunset is also good at Sentinel Bridge.

**9. Bridalveil Fall Trail — MUST**
- **Area / type:** western Valley, at the Bridalveil Fall parking area. Easy paved walk.
- **Distance / effort:** 0.5 mi round trip, about 80 ft gain, 20 min.
- **Why it's a classic:** often "the first waterfall you'll see." It is 620 ft high and flows year-round ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/bridalveilfalltrail.htm)).
- **Late-Nov status:** open all year. "Expect icy and very slick conditions in winter."
- **Parking / access:** no shuttle. Park at the trailhead lot, or along Southside Drive a few hundred yards east, which adds an alternate 0.25-mi trail. Restrooms are in the lot.
- **Pairing:** combine with Tunnel View, about 5 min away.

**10. Mirror Lake (to the lake and back) — RECOMMENDED**
- **Area / type:** eastern Valley, shuttle stop #17. Easy to moderate; the first mile is a paved service road.
- **Distance / effort:** 2 mi round trip, about 100 ft gain, about 1 hr. The full loop is 5 mi and 2–3 hr.
- **Why go:** a fresh perspective "looking up at Half Dome directly from its base" ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/mirrorlaketrail.htm)).
- **Late-Nov status:** "Little water much of the year." In late fall it is more "Mirror Meadow," so reflections may be weak. In winter the trail is "icy and snowy, especially along the south side." Do an out-and-back on the north side.
- **Parking:** none at the trailhead. Park at Curry Village or anywhere in the east Valley and take the shuttle.

**11. Yosemite Village cluster — RECOMMENDED (also the bad-weather fallback)**
- **Valley visitor center:** NPS pages call it "Yosemite Exploration Center (formerly Valley Visitor Center)," with the *Spirit of Yosemite* film and exhibits ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yv.htm)). NPS data lists it as "Yosemite Valley Welcome Center," open year-round, 9 AM–5 PM daily ([NPS API](https://developer.nps.gov/api/v1/visitorcenters?parkCode=yose)). Holiday hours are **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Yosemite Museum:** open all year, with an Indian Cultural Exhibit ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yv.htm)).
- **Ansel Adams Gallery:** **free photography walks every Tuesday and Thursday, 9 AM** from the gallery porch, limited to 10 people. Registration opens 3 days ahead on Eventbrite ([Ansel Adams Gallery](https://www.anseladams.com/photography-education/)). Whether there is a walk on Thanksgiving Day (Thu Nov 26) is **UNVERIFIED**.

### 2.3 Yosemite Valley — hikes

**12. Mist Trail: Vernal Fall footbridge → top of Vernal Fall — MUST (at least to the footbridge)**
- **Area / type:** eastern Valley, Happy Isles trailhead (shuttle #16). Moderate to the bridge, strenuous to the top ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/vernalnevadatrail.htm)).
- **Distance / effort:**

  | Goal | Round trip | Gain | Time | Difficulty |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Vernal Fall footbridge | 1.6 mi | 400 ft | 1–1.5 hr | Moderate |
  | Top of Vernal Fall (Mist Trail) | 2.4 mi | 1,000 ft | ~3 hr | Strenuous |

- **Why it's a classic:** Yosemite's signature waterfall hike. The Mist Trail climbs "a steep granite stairway of over 600 steps" to the brink of the 317-ft Vernal Fall.
- **Late-Nov status:**
  - The lower Mist Trail may be **closed for ice**. If so, use the longer JMT/Clark Point winter route (see §1.2).
  - Even when open, the steps can be icy and slick.
  - Restrooms and water at the footbridge are **May–Oct only**. Water is at the trailhead (stop #16). There are vault toilets above Vernal Fall.
  - Carry a headlamp.
- **Parking:** none at the trailhead. Park at Curry Village, under 1 mi away, early.
- **When to go** *(analysis)*: start by about 8 AM. This side of the Valley is shaded and cold in late November.

**13. Nevada Fall (via the Mist Trail) — OPTIONAL (fit groups only)**
- **Distance / effort:** 5.4 mi round trip, 2,000 ft gain, 5–6 hr, strenuous ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/vernalnevadatrail.htm)).
- **Late-Nov caveats:** short daylight; start by 8 AM so you are down well before 4:30 PM. The JMT between Clark Point and the top of Nevada Fall (the "ice cut") closes in winter. In 2024 that section closed Nov 13, so expect to return the same way.

**14. Columbia Rock (Yosemite Falls Trail, lower section) — RECOMMENDED**
- **Area / type:** Valley, Camp 4 area (shuttle #7). Moderate but steep switchbacks.
- **Distance / effort:** 2 mi round trip, 1,000 ft gain, 2–3 hr.
- **Why go:** "Spectacular views of Yosemite Valley, Half Dome, and Sentinel Rock." "Worthwhile... at any time of year" ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yosemitefallstrail.htm)).
- **Late-Nov status:** "During winter, the lower portion of this trail gets direct sunlight, so hiking can be snow-free while other trails are icy." This makes it the **best winter alternative to the Mist Trail**.
- **Parking:** Yosemite Falls parking area across from Camp 4. The Camp 4 lot is for campers only.
- **Extension:** another 0.5 mi past Columbia Rock gives a view of Upper Yosemite Fall, which will be low in late Nov.

**15. Upper Yosemite Fall (top) — OPTIONAL / generally not advised late Nov**
- **Distance / effort:** 7.2 mi round trip, 2,700 ft gain, 6–8 hr.
- **Why not:** the "upper portion... receives little to no sunlight in winter, can be very icy and slippery" ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yosemitefallstrail.htm)).

**16. Four Mile Trail — OPTIONAL**
- **Distance / effort:** 4.8 mi one way to Glacier Point (9.6 mi round trip), 3,200 ft gain, 6–8 hr round trip.
- **Late-Nov status:** likely closed or icy above Union Point; the lower 3 mi are "very icy and slippery during winter" ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/fourmiletrail.htm)). Not a first-timer priority on a short trip.

### 2.4 Giant sequoias (pick one)

**17. Mariposa Grove — RECOMMENDED (half-day detour)**
- **Area / type:** Wawona / South Entrance, 1 hr 15 min (37 mi) from the Valley ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/driving.htm)). Over 500 mature giant sequoias, the park's largest grove ([NPS sequoias](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/sequoias.htm)).
- **Access in late Nov:** a free shuttle runs from the Welcome Plaza (about 300 parking spaces). **2026 schedule, Nov 1–30:** "Weather permitting, 8 am to 3:30 pm (last bus leaves Mariposa Grove at 5:00 pm)." There is no shuttle from Dec 1 ([NPS Mariposa Grove](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/mg.htm)).
- **If the shuttle is not running:** walk the Washburn Trail or the road, **2 mi each way with about 500 ft gain**, possibly snowy or icy.
- **Trails inside the grove:**
  - **Big Trees Loop:** 0.3 mi, 30–45 min, flat, wheelchair-accessible. Passes the Fallen Monarch.
  - **Grizzly Giant Loop:** 2 mi, 1.5–2 hr, 300 ft gain. Passes the Bachelor and Three Graces, the 3,000-year-old Grizzly Giant, and the California Tunnel Tree.
- **Facilities:** water only at the Welcome Plaza. No food service.
- **Why it's a classic:** the most famous sequoia grove in the park.
- **Cost:** about 2.5 hr of driving round trip from the Valley plus 2–3 hr in the grove. The Wawona Visitor Center is closed from Nov 1 ([NPS API](https://developer.nps.gov/api/v1/visitorcenters?parkCode=yose)).

**18. Tuolumne Grove — RECOMMENDED if you enter or leave on Hwy 120 (the efficient sequoia option)**
- **Area / type:** Crane Flat, "on the Tioga Road just east of Crane Flat." About two dozen mature sequoias.
- **Distance / effort:** 1 mi down, 500 ft drop, then 1 mi back up. NPS calls the climb out "strenuous." Allow 1–1.5 hr. No water ([NPS sequoias](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/sequoias.htm)).
- **Drive:** about 45 min from the Valley, right on the Hwy 120 route.
- **Winter access:** the Tuolumne Grove and Merced Grove parking areas serve as winter trailheads, and there are vault toilets ([NPS winter activities](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/wintersports.htm)). It should therefore be reachable even after Tioga Road closes beyond Crane Flat. Check chain controls on Big Oak Flat Rd.
- **Parking:** limited.

**19. Merced Grove — OPTIONAL**
- **Area:** Big Oak Flat Rd, about 6 mi west of Crane Flat.
- **Distance / effort:** 1.5 mi down with 500 ft drop, 3 mi round trip.
- **Parking:** "extremely limited" ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/sequoias.htm)).

### 3.3 Stops genuinely worth it on the way

- **Groveland (Hwy 120):** the last real town before the Big Oak Flat entrance, about 1 hr 15 min (49 mi) from the Valley ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/driving.htm)). Gold Rush and Hetch Hetchy-era town, home of the historic Iron Door Saloon ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groveland,_California)). Good for coffee, a meal, restrooms.
  - *Caution:* the domain `iron-door-saloon.com` currently redirects to an unrelated site. Don't rely on it for hours.
- **Rim of the World vista (Hwy 120):** roadside overlook of the Tuolumne River canyon. The 2013 Rim Fire is named after it ([Wikipedia: Rim Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rim_Fire)). A 5–10 min stop. Exact location and parking details are **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Tuolumne Grove (in the park, on Hwy 120):** see #18. The best "bonus" stop on 120.
- **Mariposa (Hwy 140):** 1 hr 15 min (44 mi) from the Valley. Gold Rush town with the Mariposa Museum & History Center, the California State Mining and Mineral Museum, and the Yosemite Climbing Museum ([Yosemite Mariposa County](https://www.yosemite.com/itineraries/autumn-road-trip-hwy140/)). Good for lunch on the way home. The Merced River canyon drive on 140 is itself scenic.
- **Not worth it on this trip** *(analysis)*: going in via Hwy 41 / Fresno. NPS lists only Hwy 120 and Hwy 140 as the routes from the San Francisco Bay Area. Hwy 41 only makes sense for a plan built around Mariposa Grove.

## 4. 2026 rules: reservations, fees, non-resident surcharge, passes

### 4.1 Reservations

- **None for entry in 2026.** "Yosemite National Park today announced it will no longer use a timed reservation system in 2026" ([NPS, updated 2026-02-18](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/reservations.htm)).
- Instead the park uses real-time traffic monitoring and parking management.
- Lodging and campgrounds still need reservations; that is separate research.

### 4.2 Entrance fees ([NPS fees](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/fees.htm))

- **Private vehicle: $35**, valid 7 consecutive days, covers everyone in a car with up to 15 seats. "Per-person nonresident fees still apply."
- Motorcycle $30; per person (walk-in or bike) $20; under 16 free.
- **Cashless:** "This park does not accept cash." Credit, debit and mobile pay are accepted at the entrance stations.
- **No fee-free days during the trip.** 2026's last fee-free day was Nov 11, and from 2026 fee-free days apply to US residents only ([NPS passes](https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/passes.htm)).

### 4.3 Non-resident surcharge (new from Jan 1, 2026)

- **"Each non-US resident aged 16 and older visiting Yosemite National Park must pay a $100 nonresident fee (in addition to the standard entrance fee), unless admitted with an Annual or America the Beautiful Pass"** ([NPS fees](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/fees.htm)).
- It applies at 11 parks: Acadia, Bryce Canyon, Everglades, Glacier, Grand Canyon, Grand Teton, Rocky Mountain, Sequoia & Kings Canyon, Yellowstone, Yosemite and Zion ([NPS nonresident fees, updated 2026-07-15](https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/nonresident-fees.htm)).
- **Any valid pass covers the whole car.** "Regardless of the year issued, all valid America the Beautiful Passes and park-specific annual passes will cover entrance fees and nonresident fees for the pass holder and passengers in a private vehicle" ([NPS nonresident fees](https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/nonresident-fees.htm)). The pass holder must be present with photo ID; passes are non-transferable.

### 4.4 Who counts as a "resident"? (important for international students)

- **What NPS actually says:**
  - Proof of residency means "a U.S. Passport, U.S. government (state or territory)-issued driver's license or state ID, or Permanent Resident card ('green card')" ([NPS nonresident fees](https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/nonresident-fees.htm)).
  - Digital pass buyers "show a photo ID at time of use"; anyone found ineligible "will be asked to upgrade to the non-resident pass."
  - "Anyone may purchase the America the Beautiful Non-Resident Annual Pass" ([NPS commercial tours FAQ](https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/commercial-tours-and-nonresident-fees.htm)).
- **What NPS does not say:**
  - I found **no official NPS definition of "resident" that addresses F-1/J-1 students or other visa holders.**
  - NPS pages switch between "US citizens and residents" and "US citizens and **permanent** residents." The Yosemite Annual Pass description reads "for US citizens and permanent residents," and the passes page says "Free entrance days only apply to US citizens and permanent residents."
  - A DOI spokesperson said staff "are not checking immigration status... beyond what is necessary to confirm eligibility" ([Union Democrat/USA Today syndication, Jan 2026](https://www.uniondemocrat.com/lifestyle/article_174c43d4-273b-4463-8b82-1f44e2734869.html)).
  - A third-party guide notes that state driver's licenses are accepted at the gate, but NPS "hasn't confirmed this" for visa holders ([TripProf, updated 2026-07-03](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/us-national-park-fee-international-visitors-2026/)).
- **Bottom line:** whether international students count as residents is **UNVERIFIED and ambiguous.** The "permanent residents" wording suggests the intent is citizens plus green-card holders, which would make F-1 students non-residents. A California driver's license or ID is, however, on the list of accepted documents. The group should not misrepresent its status. If unsure, the Non-Resident pass below is the legitimate, cheapest option.

### 4.5 Passes and the cost for this group of 3 in one car

**Pass prices** ([NPS fees](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/fees.htm), [NPS passes](https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/passes.htm)):
- **Yosemite Annual Pass:** $70 (US citizens/residents).
- **America the Beautiful Resident Annual:** $80.
- **America the Beautiful Non-Resident Annual:** $250. It covers the entrance fee and all nonresident fees for the pass holder and passengers in a private vehicle (up to 15).
- Senior $20 annual / $80 lifetime; military, Access and 4th-grade passes are free.
- **Digital passes** from Recreation.gov work immediately. Physical passes ordered from the USGS store take up to 3 weeks. All passes are non-refundable.

**What the group would pay:**

| Who is in the car | Cheapest legitimate option | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| All 3 are non-residents | **Buy 1 Non-Resident ATB pass ($250)** instead of $35 + 3×$100 | **$250** (saves $85; good for 12 months at all federal sites) |
| At least 1 traveler qualifies as a resident, with accepted ID | That person buys a Yosemite Annual ($70) or ATB Resident ($80) pass; it covers the passengers' nonresident fees | **$70–80** |
| All 3 are residents | Standard vehicle pass | **$35** |

  - 8:00–10:30 **Columbia Rock**

- **Option 3, "Mariposa Grove":**
  - Leave the Valley at 8:00 → Welcome Plaza around 9:15 → shuttle (if running; "weather permitting") → Grizzly Giant Loop → back at the plaza around 12:30
  - Then Hwy 41 S → Oakhurst → Hwy 49 N → Mariposa → Hwy 140 → Merced → home. *(analysis, UNVERIFIED time)* Roughly 4–4.5 hr plus holiday traffic. A long day.

### Bad-weather / snow fallback (either plan)

1. **Before departure:**
   - Check 209/372-0200 (1, 1), Caltrans 800/427-7623 and the NWS forecast.
   - With a storm in the forecast, take **Hwy 140** both ways.
   - Have chains that fit your tires (they are hard to rent; a few are sold at the Village Garage and the Wawona gas station). Know how to install them.
   - Remember: no gas in the Valley ([NPS chains](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/tirechains.htm), [driving](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/driving.htm)).
   - Shoe traction devices are a good idea *(analysis)*.
2. **Still good in rain or snow:**
   - Tunnel View, Valley View and El Capitan Meadow, all from the car. A clearing storm is the best photography of the year *(analysis)*.
   - Cook's Meadow (frosty boardwalks are slippery) and Lower Yosemite Fall.
   - The **Valley Floor Tour** (bus), the visitor center film and exhibits, the Yosemite Museum and the Ansel Adams Gallery.
   - Ice skating, when conditions permit.
3. **Swap out when icy:**
   - Mist Trail steps → stop at the Vernal Fall footbridge.
   - Mirror Lake loop → north-side out and back only.
   - Upper Yosemite Falls Trail and Four Mile Trail → skip.
   - Columbia Rock is usually the best winter hike (sunny).
4. **Mariposa Grove:** if the shuttle isn't running, it's a 4-mi round-trip walk on the Washburn Trail plus the grove loops. Choose Tuolumne Grove instead if you are on Hwy 120, or skip sequoias.
5. **A major storm on the drive day:** the Valley stays open by car all year ([NPS winter](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/winter.htm)). If chain controls go up and you can't chain up, NPS suggests parking at a YARTS stop on Hwy 140 and taking the bus in for a fee ([NPS chains](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/tirechains.htm)). YARTS winter schedules were not researched.
