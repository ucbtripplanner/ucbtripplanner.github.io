# Yosemite Thanksgiving 2026 — Logistics, Rules, Safety & Prep

Research date: **Sat 2026-11-07**. Trip: 2 nights / 3 days within **Thu Nov 26 – Sun Nov 29, 2026** (Thu–Sat or Fri–Sun), 3 adults (likely incl. international students), own 2WD car on all-season tires, from Berkeley.

Legend: **[Official]** = NPS / Caltrans / NWS / NOAA / USNO page fetched on 2026-11-07. **[Unverified for 2026]** = latest info found is older or not explicitly confirmed. "Last updated" dates are the dates printed on the source page.

## TL;DR

- **No reservation / timed entry in 2026.** Just pay the entrance fee at the gate (card only). [Official]
- **Fees:** $35 per car (7 days). **Non-US residents age 16+ pay an extra $100 each** at Yosemite since Jan 1 2026 — **unless the car enters on an annual / America the Beautiful (ATB) pass, which covers everyone in the car.** ATB passes: $80 Resident / $250 Non-Resident. [Official]
- **Cheapest legal option for this group:** if anyone in the car has a US passport, green card, or **US state-issued driver's license / state ID** (the documents NPS lists), that person buys the **$80 Resident ATB pass** → it covers the car and all passengers' nonresident fees. If nobody has those documents → **$250 Non-Resident ATB pass** (cheaper than $35 + 3 × $100 = $335).
- **Route:** Hwy 120 (Big Oak Flat) is shortest in dry weather but tops out at ~6,190 ft (Crane Flat); **Hwy 140 (Arch Rock) never goes above ~4,000 ft — the bad-weather route** — switch to it if snow/chain controls are forecast. Hwy 41 is longest from Berkeley; skip unless visiting Mariposa Grove.
- **Chains:** NPS says from **Nov–Mar expect chain requirements**; when they're in effect **all vehicles must carry chains, including 4WD and rentals.** Buy chains that fit your tire size *before* leaving (e.g., Les Schwab Livermore on I-580 — full refund if unused, returned at end of season).
- **Weather:** Valley daily normals for Nov 26–29: **high ~50 °F / low ~30 °F**. In ~5 of the last ~24 years with data, the Valley had measurable snow on Nov 26–29. NOAA's Week 3–4 outlook (Nov 21–Dec 4) **favors above-normal precipitation for much of California** (El Niño year).
- **Daylight:** sunrise ~6:50 am, sunset ~4:41 pm (≈9 h 50 m of daylight). Bring headlamps.
- **No gas in Yosemite Valley.** Fill up in the Central Valley (Oakdale/Manteca/Merced) before the mountains.
- **Traffic:** Thanksgiving Day itself has the lightest traffic; the worst is the Wed before and the Sat/Sun afternoons. **Thu–Sat is the easier traffic option.**

### Recommendation for late November
1. **Default: in via Hwy 120** if the forecast is dry and no chain controls are posted on Big Oak Flat Rd.
2. **Switch to Hwy 140** if snow is forecast, chain controls (R1/R2) are posted on Hwy 120/Big Oak Flat Rd, or you'll be driving after dark. It's only ~20 mi longer, lowest elevation, and it's the route YARTS runs year-round.
3. A common combo: **in via 120, out via 140** (or vice versa) — similar total time, different scenery.
4. Avoid Hwy 41 from Berkeley unless the Mariposa Grove is in the plan (Grove shuttle runs **Nov 1–30, 8 am–3:30 pm; last bus leaves the grove 5 pm** — Yosemite Guide Sep 23–Nov 24 2026).
5. **Tioga Road (Hwy 120 east) and Glacier Point Road** were still open on Nov 6 2026 (no overnight parking), but both "typically close for the winter sometime in November" — **assume they may be closed by Thanksgiving.** https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm

**Practical timing**

## 2. Park entry in late November 2026

### Reservations
- **No reservation or timed-entry permit in 2026.** "Yosemite National Park today announced it will no longer use a timed reservation system in 2026." The park instead uses real-time traffic monitoring, active Valley parking management, extra staffing at intersections, and congestion alerts. Last updated Feb 18 2026. https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/reservations.htm
- FAQ: "You do not need a reservation to enter Yosemite in 2026. The park entrance fee still applies when you arrive." https://www.nps.gov/yose/faqs.htm
- (Lodging/campsites still need their own reservations — separate topic.)

| Pass | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Private vehicle (7 days) | **$35** | Covers "the vehicle and everyone inside of it" (entrance fee only — see nonresident fee below) |
| Motorcycle (7 days) | $30 | |

- Only November fee-free day in 2026: **Nov 11 (Veterans Day)** — and since 2026 fee-free days apply **only to US residents**. Nothing free over Thanksgiving. https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/nonresident-fees.htm

### The 2026 nonresident fee — details (NPS "Nonresident Fees" page, last updated Jul 15 2026: https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/nonresident-fees.htm)
- Effective **Jan 1 2026**, from the Executive Order "Making America Beautiful Again by Improving Our National Parks." Parks: Acadia, Bryce Canyon, Everglades, Glacier, Grand Canyon, Grand Teton, Rocky Mountain, Sequoia & Kings Canyon, Yellowstone, **Yosemite**, Zion.
- **How residency is proven:** "To purchase an America the Beautiful Resident Annual Pass, a visitor will need to show proof of U.S. citizenship or residency." **"Acceptable documents include a U.S. Passport, U.S. government (state or territory)-issued driver's license or state ID, or Permanent Resident card."**
- **Digital passes:** "Purchasers will show a photo ID at time of use."
- **Coverage of passengers (key for this group):** "Regardless of the year issued, **all valid America the Beautiful Passes and park-specific annual passes will cover entrance fees and nonresident fees for the pass holder and passengers in a private vehicle** or on two motorcycles." The NPS passes page says the Non-Resident pass covers the pass holder and passengers in a private vehicle (up to 15 passengers). https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/passes.htm (updated Oct 9 2026)
- ATB passes are "non-refundable, non-transferable" and "Passholders must show valid photo identification (ID) with each pass." https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/passes.htm
- DOI announcement (Nov 25 2025): https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1207/department-of-the-interior-announces-modernized-more-affordable-national-park-access.htm

### What this means for international students (e.g., F-1)
- **NPS does not define "resident" by visa status anywhere we could find.** None of the official pages (Yosemite fees, NPS passes, NPS nonresident-fees, DOI announcement) mention visas, F-1/J-1, or students. What they *do* specify is the **document list**: US passport, **US state/territory-issued driver's license or state ID**, or green card. → An international student who holds a **California driver's license or CA ID card** appears to meet the documented proof for the $80 Resident ATB pass. **[Gray area — not explicitly confirmed by NPS for visa holders; enforcement practice at the gate unverified.]**
- Note the **Yosemite-only $70 annual pass** is worded more narrowly ("US citizens and **permanent** residents") → students on visas should **not** rely on that pass.
- A university ID or foreign passport is **not** on the list → a student with only a passport/visa is treated as a nonresident.

**Cost scenarios for one car, 3 adults (all 16+):**
| Situation | Cheapest compliant option | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| ≥1 person has US passport / green card / **US state DL or state ID** | That person buys the **$80 Resident ATB** (online at recreation.gov or at the gate) and is in the car with photo ID | **$80 total** (covers the car + all passengers' nonresident fees) |
| All 3 are US residents with ID, only this one trip | $35 vehicle pass | $35 |
| Nobody has a qualifying document | **$250 Non-Resident ATB** (covers whole car) | **$250** (vs. $35 + 3×$100 = $335) |
| Pay-as-you-go, 2 nonresidents + 1 resident | $35 + 2×$100 | $235 (worse than $80 ATB) |

- Buy: entrance stations (card only), **digital ATB pass at https://www.recreation.gov/pass/** ("can be saved to a mobile device and used immediately"), or USGS store (physical, up to 3 weeks delivery — too slow now). Screenshot/download the digital pass before leaving (no cell service at entrances is likely — see §5).
- Bonus: an ATB pass is valid 12 months at all federal recreation sites (useful for later trips, e.g., Pinnacles, Muir Woods, Sequoia & Kings Canyon).

## 3. Winter driving & tire chains

### Yosemite rules (NPS "Tire Chains" page, last updated Dec 3 2025 — https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/tirechains.htm)
- "If you are visiting any location in Yosemite **from November through March, you should expect chain requirements to be in effect**" (occasionally Sept–May).
- When restrictions are in effect, "**all vehicles must have chains in possession, including four-wheel drive and rental vehicles**." The 2026 Yosemite Guide repeats this: "ALL vehicles must carry chains, including 4-wheel drive and rental vehicles."
- Legal devices: **chains, cables, and tire socks.** "Plastic devices, straps, and other emergency or alternative 'tire chains' do not meet the legal requirement."
- Fine: "subject to a citation (**up to $5,000**) if you fail to put on chains when required."
- Buying: "most auto parts stores and gas stations" near the park; **limited selection at the Village Garage (Yosemite Valley) and Wawona gas station**; "It's difficult to find businesses that rent chains; they're usually only available for purchase."
- Yosemite Village Garage (2026 fall Guide): 8 am–12 pm, 1–5 pm; **24-hour roadside assistance 209/372-1060**.

### Chain control levels (Caltrans — https://dot.ca.gov/travel/winter-driving-tips/chain-controls)
| Level | Meaning | Our 2WD car on all-season tires |
|---|---|---|
| **R-1** | "Chains are required on all vehicles except passenger vehicles and light-duty trucks under 6,000 pounds gross weight and equipped with snow tires on at least two drive wheels." Vehicles with snow tires must still carry chains. | All-season tires marked **M+S / M/S / "MUD AND SNOW"** count as "snow-tread tires" under CA Vehicle Code §558 → **check your sidewall.** If M+S on the drive wheels: may drive without chains in R-1 (still must carry). If not M+S: **chains on.** |
| **R-2** | "Chains or traction devices are required on all vehicles except four-wheel/all-wheel drive vehicles with snow-tread tires on all four wheels." | **Chains on** (2WD). |
| **R-3** | "Chains or traction devices are required on all vehicles, no exceptions." Rare; highways usually close first. | Chains on / road likely closed. |

- NPS's version: R-1 exempts autos/pickups < 6,000 lb with snow tires on drive wheels (not towing); R-2 exempts 4WD < 6,500 lb with snow tires on all four wheels and 4WD engaged. https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/tirechains.htm
- Caltrans: "The speed limit when chains are required is **25 or 30 miles an hour**." Install on the **drive wheels** (front on most FWD cars). Chain installers on highways are "independent business people, not Caltrans employees," and "are NOT allowed to sell or rent chains" — get a receipt and badge number. https://dot.ca.gov/travel/winter-driving-tips
- Check your **owner's manual** for chain restrictions/clearance (some cars require low-profile "Class S" chains or socks) — general advice, verify for your car.

### Where to buy (and return) chains
- **Les Schwab** — policy: "If your quick-fit chains, tire socks, or other traction devices remain unused at the end of the season, bring them back to any Les Schwab for a **full refund in the spring**"; "Full Refund for Unused Passenger or Light Truck Tire Chains" (must be properly stored, not rusted). Free in-store fitting/lesson; text "CHAINS" to 52225 for an install video. https://www.lesschwab.com/more/tire-chains
  - On/near the route: **Livermore, 2650 Las Positas Rd (925) 245-9995** (right off I-580); Concord, Fremont, **Tracy** 3260 W Grant Line Rd, **Manteca** 1240 W Yosemite Ave, **Oakdale** 1300 E F St (Hwy 120), **Merced** 430 W 16th St (Hwy 140), Modesto. https://www.lesschwab.com/stores/ca
  - Note: refund is at **end of season (spring)**, not immediately after the trip.
- Other options: auto-parts chains (AutoZone, O'Reilly), Walmart, Costco, gas stations in Oakdale/Groveland/Mariposa/Oakhurst. **Their return policies for unused chains were not verifiable** (sites blocked) — keep the receipt and do not open/use the package if you want to return; confirm policy at purchase. Gateway-town and in-park prices are typically higher and stock (sizes) can run out right before storms (general expectation, unverified).
- **Know your tire size** (sidewall, e.g., 215/55R17) before shopping. **Practice installing once at home** in dry conditions — first time in the snow, in the dark, is miserable.
- Rentals: NPS says chain rental is rare. **[No verified rental source found.]**

## 4. Late-November weather in Yosemite Valley

### Normals and history
- **Daily normals (1991–2020), Yosemite Park Headquarters (4,018 ft, in the Valley):** Nov 26: **51 °F / 30 °F**; Nov 27–29: **50 °F / 30 °F**; normal daily precip ≈ 0.16–0.19 in. Source: NOAA Regional Climate Centers ACIS (station COOP 049855) https://data.rcc-acis.org/ (query: StnData sid=049855, normal=1).
- NPS monthly averages for the Valley (Nov): high 56 °F / low 33 °F, 4.1 in precip (whole-month average; late Nov is colder than the month mean). Tuolumne Meadows (8,600 ft) Nov: 47/20 °F. "Most of Yosemite is blanketed in snow from about November through May"; the Valley gets rain or snow in winter storms. Updated Oct 20 2024. https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/weather.htm
- **Historical Nov 26–29 at Yosemite HQ (ACIS daily data, 1995–2025, our analysis):**
  - Measurable precipitation on at least one of those 4 days in **14 of 29 years** with data (~half).
  - Measurable **snowfall in the Valley in 5 of ~24 years** with snow data (2001, 2015, 2016, 2019, 2024); Thanksgiving 2019 brought **9–14 in/day, ~22 in snow depth**.
  - Range observed: highs 32–68 °F, lows down to 19 °F.
  - → Plan for **mostly 40s–50s °F days, freezing nights, roughly a coin-flip chance of some rain, and a real (≈1-in-5) chance of snow on the Valley floor**. Higher roads (Crane Flat ~6,200 ft, Hwy 41 near Chinquapin) see snow more often than the Valley.
- **2026 outlook:** NOAA CPC **Week 3–4 outlook (valid Sat Nov 21 – Fri Dec 4 2026, issued Nov 6 2026):** "Above-normal temperatures are also favored across the West Coast states" and "the highest probabilities for above-normal precipitation extend across much of California," driven by an ongoing **El Niño**. Next update Fri Nov 13 2026. Low-skill forecast — treat as "be ready for storms." https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/predictions/WK34/ (text: https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/predictions/WK34/texts/week34fcst.txt)
- Current (Nov 7 2026) NWS forecast for the Valley: sunny, highs ~69–75 °F through Nov 12, 30 % rain chance Nov 13 — unusually warm, but too early to say anything about Thanksgiving.

## 5. Practical info

### Gas (2026 fall Yosemite Guide + NPS driving page)
- "**Gas is NOT available in Yosemite Valley.**" Gas is no longer sold in Tuolumne Meadows. https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/driving.htm
- In/near the park, all **24-hour pay-at-the-pump, card only**, gas & diesel:
  - **El Portal** (outside park on Hwy 140) — 15 mi from Valley (downhill); staffed 8 am–5 pm.
  - **Crane Flat** (Hwy 120/Big Oak Flat Rd) — 16.5 mi from Valley (uphill); station open year-round (store closed Oct 25).
  - **Wawona** (Hwy 41) — 28 mi from Valley (uphill); staffed 9 am–5 pm.
  Source: Yosemite Guide Sep 23–Nov 24 2026 (PDF) https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/upload/Yosemite-Guide-51-8-508V1.pdf
- **Last cheaper gas:** Central Valley metros were cheapest on Nov 7 2026 (AAA regular avg): **Modesto $5.56, Stockton-Lodi $5.60**, Merced $5.70, Fresno $5.71, vs Oakland $5.76, statewide $5.76. https://gasprices.aaa.com/?state=CA → fill up in **Manteca/Oakdale (Hwy 120)** or **Merced (Hwy 140)** / Madera–Fresno (Hwy 41). Mountain-town and in-park prices were not verifiable (GasBuddy blocked) — typically higher.
- Tip (general, unverified for these pumps): US pay-at-pump terminals often ask for a billing ZIP code; **non-US cards may fail** → use a US-issued card, or pay inside during staffed hours.
- Arrive with a full-ish tank: idling in storm/holiday traffic and heater use burn fuel.

- **Before leaving:** download Google Maps offline area (Yosemite + Hwy 120/140 corridors), download the **NPS App** offline content for Yosemite, save the digital pass, lodging confirmations and a screenshot of the Valley shuttle map. Don't trust GPS shortcuts (NPS warning).

### Parking in the Valley on a holiday weekend
- NPS traffic page (updated Oct 27 2026): Yosemite Valley — "**arrive before 8 am**, after which parking is usually full" on busy days; worst on "Saturdays and holiday weekends." https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/traffic.htm
- 2026 Guide: three main day-use hubs — **Yosemite Village (Stop 1), Yosemite Falls (Stop 7), Curry Village (Stops 14/19)**. "Parking can completely fill by late morning on weekends and holidays. **Once you find a parking space, keep it.**" Park only in marked spaces or paved turnouts fully off the road; cars on vegetation get cited/towed.
- 2026 has **no reservation system**; NPS uses "active parking management" instead (rangers may redirect traffic). https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/reservations.htm
- **Sleeping in a car is only allowed at a registered campsite**; no overnight sleeping in parking areas/roadsides. No overnight parking on Tioga & Glacier Point Roads (since Oct 15). (2026 Guide; NPS alerts)

### Shuttles in late November
- **Yosemite Valley shuttles are free, 7 am–10 pm daily, year-round, no ticket.** Fall 2026 Guide: **Valleywide (green)** serves all stops, approx. **every 22–32 min** (1.5 h loop); **East Valley (purple)** approx. **every 18–22 min** (50 min loop). (NPS web page, updated Jul 2 2026, lists 12–22 / 8–12 min — the Guide's fall figures are more conservative; check the new Guide.) https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/publictransportation.htm
- **Mariposa Grove shuttle:** Nov 1–30, 8 am–3:30 pm (last bus leaves the grove 5 pm). Grove road open only to disability-placard holders. (2026 Guide; NPS conditions)
- **YARTS Hwy 140** (Merced–Mariposa–Valley) runs **all year** — a snow-day fallback (park in Mariposa/Merced). Other YARTS routes are summer-only. Schedules/fares: https://yarts.com/ **[Thanksgiving-day schedule unverified]**
- Pets not allowed on shuttles.

### Services you might not expect to be closed
- Late fall = "reduced visitor services": many shops/restaurants close or cut hours; **Glacier Point Road/Tioga Road may close**; **Half Dome cables are down**; **waterfalls may be a trickle or dry** in fall (rain or early snow can revive them). Curry Village **ice rink opens ~Nov 13 if conditions allow** (sessions 12 pm, 3:30 pm, 7 pm; +8:30 am weekends). Valley Welcome Center 9 am–5 pm. (2026 Guide)
- **Winter trail closures:** the **Mist Trail above the Vernal Fall Footbridge** and the JMT between Clark Point and the Panorama Trail junction **"may close in November"**; the **upper Four Mile Trail closes in winter.** (2026 Guide) Dome Fire (Sept–Oct 2026) closures remain for an area south of Glacier Point Road toward Bridalveil Creek; no fire restrictions in effect as of Nov 6. https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm

### Bears & food storage (required by federal law)
- **Daytime:** "During daylight hours _only_, you may store food _inside_ your car or truck (out of sight, with windows completely closed)." Keep food "within arm's reach" when hiking/picnicking; never leave it unattended. https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/bears.htm (updated Mar 14 2024)
- **At night:** "Food*, trash, and other scented items may **NOT** be stored inside vehicles overnight." Use a **bear-proof food locker**, your hotel room / hard-sided cabin. (2026 Guide) Lockers are at "Curry Village parking lots and at nearly all trailhead parking areas" plus every campsite, Housekeeping unit, and Curry tent cabin.
- **"Food" = anything with a scent:** canned goods, drinks, **toiletries, soap, cosmetics, sunscreen, trash, recycling, unwashed dishes, ice chests/coolers (even empty)**. Clean out the car (wrappers, coffee cups) before arriving.
- Never in a truck bed or strapped outside the vehicle.
- Penalty: "impoundment of your food or car and/or a **fine of up to $5,000** and/or removal from your campsite or lodging facility."
- Distances: **at least 150 ft (50 yd) from bears and coyotes; 25 ft from other wildlife**; it's illegal to approach or feed any wildlife (incl. squirrels, birds, deer). If a bear approaches you in a developed area, yell aggressively to scare it off. Report sightings: **Save-a-Bear hotline 209/372-0322** or yose_bear_mgmt@nps.gov; bear in food/refusing to leave → 911. https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/scarebears.htm
- "27 bears were hit by vehicles in Yosemite last year" — obey speed limits, "Red Bear, Dead Bear" signs. (2026 Guide)

### Other park rules / safety (2026 Guide; NPS safety page updated Dec 3 2025 — https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/safety.htm)
- **Emergency: call or text 911.** General info 209/372-0200. Yosemite Medical Clinic 209/372-4637. Roadside assistance 209/372-1060.
- **Drones prohibited.** Marijuana (incl. medical) prohibited (federal land). No collecting plants/rocks/artifacts. Pets leashed, not on trails/shuttles/buildings. Campfires only in established rings/grills.
- Stream-polished rocks near water are slippery wet or dry; never wade above a waterfall; stay behind barriers at waterfall bases; rockfall happens several times a year in the Valley.

### Leave No Trace — 7 principles (https://www.nps.gov/articles/leave-no-trace-seven-principles.htm)
1. **Plan ahead & prepare** (know regulations, weather, emergencies).
2. **Travel on durable surfaces** (stay on trails; don't trample meadows — also illegal to drive/park on them).
3. **Dispose of waste properly** — "Pack it in, pack it out."
4. **Leave what you find** (rocks, pinecones, artifacts stay).
5. **Minimize campfire impacts** (use existing rings; fully extinguish).
6. **Respect wildlife** — observe from a distance; "Never feed animals"; store food securely.
7. **Be considerate of other visitors** (yield on trails, keep noise down).

## 6. Packing checklist — late-Nov Yosemite, first-timers

**Clothing (layering; 30 °F mornings → 50s °F afternoons, possible rain/snow)**
- [ ] Base layer top (synthetic/merino; no cotton for hikes)
- [ ] Fleece or light insulating mid-layer
- [ ] **Warm puffy jacket** (evenings/mornings near or below freezing)
- [ ] **Waterproof rain shell** (and rain pants if storms forecast) — NPS: pack "warm and waterproof clothing layers"
- [ ] Warm hat (beanie) + gloves; buff/neck gaiter
- [ ] Hiking pants (not jeans if rain); thermal leggings for nights
- [ ] **Waterproof hiking shoes/boots with good tread** + wool socks (+ a dry spare pair)
- [ ] Comfortable layers/clothes for lodging; sleepwear for cold rooms/cabins

**Traction & trail gear**
- [ ] **Microspikes / slip-on traction** for icy trails and shaded paths (Mist Trail steps, Lower Yosemite Fall area, mornings after a freeze) — buy before the trip at an outdoor store (e.g., REI); the Curry Village Mountain Shop sells hiking/climbing gear (2026 Guide) but stock is unverified
- [ ] Trekking poles (optional, helpful on icy/wet granite)
- [ ] **Headlamp per person + spare batteries** — sunset ~4:40 pm (NPS lists headlamp as essential)
- [ ] Day pack, paper **map** (Guide map) — phones lose signal
- [ ] Water: NPS guidance "minimum 1 qt/L per person for every 2 hours on the trail"; insulated bottle / thermos for hot drinks
- [ ] Salty snacks, lunch (keep within arm's reach; lockers at night)
- [ ] Sunglasses + sunscreen + lip balm (low sun, snow glare) — remember: scented items go in lockers at night
- [ ] Small first-aid kit, hand warmers, blister care

**Car kit (winter)**
- [ ] **Tire chains/cables/socks that fit your tire size** (required to carry Nov–Mar when posted) + gloves, kneeling pad/trash bag, flashlight for installing
- [ ] Ice scraper/brush, windshield washer fluid rated for freezing
- [ ] Phone car charger / power bank
- [ ] Blanket or sleeping bag, extra water & snacks (in case of storm delays/closures)
- [ ] Check tire tread & pressure, wipers, battery; full tank before the mountains
- [ ] Trash bags (pack out; also helps clean food smells out of the car)

**Documents & money**
- [ ] **Photo ID for every adult**; for the pass holder: US passport / green card / US state DL or ID (to buy the Resident ATB pass)
- [ ] International students: passport (+ I-20/visa docs per your school's travel guidance), driver's license + insurance/registration for the car
- [ ] **US-issued credit/debit card** (park entrance and gas pumps are card-only)
- [ ] Digital ATB pass saved offline; lodging confirmations offline
