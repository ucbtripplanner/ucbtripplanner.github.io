Research date: **Tue 11/10 – Wed 11/11/2026**. Trip: **Thu 11/26 to Sat 11/28/2026** (2 nights). 3 adults (maybe 4), Korean students at UC Berkeley (some may be international students). Own regular 2WD car, Berkeley → Mendocino coast → Berkeley.

## TL;DR

- **Three sensible routes** (OSRM free-flow, no stops; real trips run longer):
  - **A. US-101 → CA-128 (Anderson Valley) → CA-1.** Berkeley → Mendocino village is **155 mi, about 3 h 35 min**. This is the standard, fastest and least scary route, with redwoods and an apple and wine valley on the way.
  - **B. Coast: US-101 → Bodega Bay → Jenner → CA-1 north** via Fort Ross, Sea Ranch, Gualala and Point Arena. **166 mi, about 4 h 10 min** with no stops, so plan **6–7 h** with photo stops. From Jenner to Mendocino it averages only **~34 mph** over 91 mi of curves.
  - **C. Return via CA-20 → Willits → US-101.** From **Fort Bragg it is 173 mi, about 3 h 50 min**, which is about as fast as CA-128 from Fort Bragg. It's the straightest freeway-heavy option and the best fallback if CA-128 floods.
- **Suggested combo** (my synthesis): go up the **coast (B) on Thanksgiving Thu**, when traffic is minimal and the ocean sits across the opposite lane. Come home on **CA-128 (A) or CA-20 (C) Sat morning**. If anyone gets carsick, or the weather is stormy, use **A both ways**.
  - **Catch:** the **Point Arena Lighthouse is closed on Thanksgiving Day** ("We are closed on Thanksgiving and Christmas days", per an Apr-2026 snapshot of its site; the live site was rate-limited, see `sights.md`). On a Thursday coast drive you'd only see it from outside. If the lighthouse matters, flip the loop: take **A out on Thu** and **B home on Sat** (lighthouse 10 am–3:30 pm). That means a longer, later Saturday drive into the 1–8 pm traffic window, with the last hour in the dark (§1.6).
- **Daylight is short:** sunset is **4:53–4:55 pm** on the coast, and it's dark by **~5:23 pm** (civil dusk). The Moon rises only after **7–9:30 pm**, so the early evening is pitch-black on unlit two-lane roads. **Be off CA-1/CA-128 curves by ~5 pm.**
- **Traffic (AAA/INRIX 2025 pattern; the 2026 forecast isn't out yet, as of 11/11):** Thanksgiving Day had "minimal traffic impact". On Sat the worst window was **1–8 pm** and the best was **before 10 am**. AAA's 2025 Bay Area hotspot was **SF → Santa Rosa on US-101 N, Tue 7:15 pm: 2 h 34 min, +114 %**. That's the corridor this trip uses, so **don't leave Wednesday evening**.
- **Tolls:** only the **Richmond–San Rafael Bridge, westbound: $8.50** per car. It's cash-free: pay by FasTrak, by license plate (a short-term plate account up to 30 days ahead or within 48 h after crossing), or by the mailed invoice. Eastbound on the way home is free. You'd pay the **Golden Gate** only if you come home through SF: **$10.25 FasTrak / $10.50 plate or one-time / $11.25 invoice**, southbound only. There are **no express lanes on the route**, so a FasTrak tag isn't required.
- **Gas:** the CA average for regular is **$5.71** (AAA, 11/10); Santa Rosa $5.81, Oakland $5.70. Stations are **sparse**:
  - **Fill up in Cloverdale or Healdsburg/Santa Rosa** before CA-128, in **Gualala** on CA-1, and in **Fort Bragg before CA-20**, which has no station for 33 mi to Willits.
  - Budget about **$75–100 per car** for ~380 mi (≈ $25–33 per person).
- **Roads right now** (Caltrans, 11/10 11:55 pm) [Official]:
  - **CA-1 has one-way traffic control at a rock slide 14.8–16.5 mi north of Point Arena** (≈2–4 mi south of Elk; up to 10 min delays).
  - **Westport** (north of Fort Bragg) has 24/7 one-way control through 12/31.
  - A batch of utility-work signals near Little River, Mendocino and Fort Bragg runs through 11/13 only.
  - CA-128 in Mendocino County, CA-20 and CA-253 are all clear.
- **Winter storm risk is real this year.**
  - **Above-normal precipitation is favored for California for 11/21–12/4**: an El Niño pattern (NOAA CPC week 3–4 outlook, issued 11/6).
  - **CA-128 floods at the Navarro River.** The NWS gauge says **at 23 ft, "full highway closure"**. It hit that level in **Jan 2026, Feb 2025, Feb 2024, Mar and Jan 2023**.
  - When it floods, **use CA-20 (Fort Bragg ↔ Willits)**.
  - Storms also bring **PG&E outages, downed trees and slides on CA-1**.
- **Ocean:** **Thu 11/26 is an official California King Tide day** (statewide dates 11/24–26). High tides of **~7.5 ft** come around **11 am–1 pm**, and the minus tides come only after dark.
  - NWS Eureka: "the general public should simply stay out of the water along the north coast of California."
  - **Never turn your back on the ocean. Stay off rocks and logs, and well above the highest wet sand.**
- **Cell service** drops out on long stretches: CA-128 through the redwoods, CA-1 between Jenner and Gualala, and CA-20 (carrier specifics **UNVERIFIED**). **Download offline maps** before leaving.
- **Nearest ER:**
  - **Adventist Health Mendocino Coast, 700 River Dr, Fort Bragg (707-961-1234)**, ~16 min from Mendocino village.
  - There is **no hospital on the coast between Santa Rosa and Fort Bragg**: from Gualala it's ~1.5 h to an ER either way.
  - From Boonville the nearest is **Ukiah** (~35 min).
- **Day-use fees** (CA State Parks, 11/11):
  - **Mendocino Headlands: free.**
  - **$10 per car:** Russian Gulch, Van Damme, Hendy Woods, Fort Ross, Salt Point, Sonoma Coast.
  - **$8:** MacKerricher (Glass Beach area).
  - Point Cabrillo Light Station requests a **$5 donation**. Its museum is open 11–4 **daily including holidays**.
  - Sea Ranch coastal-access trails are **free** (Sonoma County Parks).

### 1.2 Route A: US-101 → CA-128 (Anderson Valley)
- **Why:** it's the fastest way to the village, it has the most services (Cloverdale, Boonville, Philo, Navarro all have gas per OSM, §5), and it has the most worthwhile inland stops (§2). It's also the least exposed to the ocean.
- **CA-128 profile:**
  - "predominantly two lanes": it climbs out of the Russian River valley over the Yorkville Highlands, runs through Anderson Valley vineyards and orchards, then follows the Navarro River through coast redwoods to CA-1 [Secondary, [Wikipedia SR-128](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_128)].
  - Average speed ≈ 39–40 mph by OSRM [Data].
- **Main hazard:** "The section of Route 128 through Navarro River Redwoods State Park is often closed in winter storms due to flooding." The alternates are the county roads Philo–Greenwood Rd (to Elk) and Comptche–Ukiah Rd [Secondary, Wikipedia]. Both are narrow, unlit back roads, so in a storm **CA-20 is the better detour**. The NWS gauge thresholds are in §8.

### 1.3 Route B: CA-1 along the Sonoma and Mendocino coast
- **Why:** this is the iconic coastal drive: Bodega Bay, the Russian River mouth at Jenner, Fort Ross, Sea Ranch and the Point Arena Lighthouse.
- **Cost:** **+30–40 min of pure driving** vs Route A, but realistically **+2–3 h** with stops. The road north of Jenner has tight hairpins and steep drop-offs, with long stretches of no services and weak signal. It's the **worst route for motion sickness**.
- **Direction matters** (geometry, my note): **northbound, you drive in the inland lane** and the ocean is beyond the oncoming lane. **Southbound, your lane is the cliff-side lane.** Passengers get better views southbound, but the driver is on the exposed edge, and it's the slower choice on a Saturday return that's already racing the sunset and the 1–8 pm traffic window.
- **Timing:** start the Jenner → Mendocino stretch by **~noon** to reach the village before sunset (4:54 pm).

### 1.4 Route C: CA-20 → Willits → US-101
- CA-20 between Fort Bragg and Willits is 33 mi of winding road through Jackson Demonstration State Forest, at about 39 mph average [Data]. NWS Eureka lists its high point **"Five miles west of Willits (2000 ft)"**. The US-101 high point south of Willits is **Ridgewood Summit (1956 ft)** [Official, [NWS EKA mountain pass forecasts](https://www.weather.gov/eka/mountainPassForecast)].
- Snow at ~2,000 ft is **rare but possible in a cold late-November storm**. NWS Eureka publishes pass forecasts for exactly these two points, so check them if a cold system is forecast. Chain controls on these passes are **UNVERIFIED for any given storm** (check Caltrans).
- **From Fort Bragg, CA-20 ≈ CA-128 in time** (≈ 3 h 50 min vs ≈ 4 h) [Data]. From Mendocino village, CA-20 adds ~25 min.

### 1.5 Golden Gate option (not needed)
Going through SF (Bay Bridge westbound, then Golden Gate northbound) only makes sense if you want a Golden Gate photo stop. The Golden Gate is **tolled southbound only**, so coming home that way costs $10.25–11.25 (§4). The Bay Bridge's 2026 toll schedule wasn't checked (**UNVERIFIED**). The default Richmond–San Rafael route is shorter and cheaper.

### 1.6 Suggested route combinations (my synthesis)
1. **Classic first-timer loop:**
   - **Thu (out):** Route **B** (coast).
     - Leave Berkeley **~8:00 am**.
     - Bodega Bay ~9:40. Jenner ~10:15.
     - Fort Ross, Sea Ranch and Gualala around midday. Point Arena ~1:30–2:30: the town, Arena Cove and the Stornetta bluffs. **The lighthouse itself is closed on Thanksgiving** (Apr-2026 site snapshot, via `sights.md`).      - Mendocino by ~4:00–4:30 pm.
     - Thanksgiving Day roads are quiet, and you drive the inland lane.
   - **Sat (home):** Route **A** via Anderson Valley.
     - Leave Mendocino **~9–9:30 am**.
     - Hendy Woods or Philo stops ~10–11:30.
     - Healdsburg lunch ~12:30.
     - Berkeley ~3 pm.
     - Or take Route **C** for the fastest return.
   - **Flipped version (lighthouse open):**
     - **Thu:** take Route **A** out.
     - **Sat:** take Route **B** home. Leave Mendocino ~9:30–10 am. The Point Arena Lighthouse (10–3:30) is ~1 h 08 min south of the village (OSRM, per `sights.md`).
     - Leave the Point Arena area by ~2 pm. From there it's ~3 h to Berkeley (OSRM). You'll get back ~5:30–6:30 pm, with the Jenner → Bodega stretch near or after sunset (4:53). This leg also falls inside the Sat 1–8 pm traffic window.
     - Southbound, the driver is in the cliff-side lane (§1.3).
2. **Low-stress / carsick-prone / stormy forecast:** Route **A both ways**. See the coast as day trips from the village (Point Cabrillo, Russian Gulch, Van Damme, Glass Beach), or drive CA-1 south only as far as Elk and back.
3. **If CA-128 is closed at the Navarro:** in and out by **Route C (CA-20)**, or Route B for the coast. Check Caltrans (§15).

## 7. Driving the curvy roads: deer, darkness, fog, rain, motion sickness

- **Darkness:**
  - Sunset ~4:54 pm, civil dusk ~5:23 pm. The waning Moon rises late (Thu 7:01 pm, Fri 8:18 pm, Sat 9:35 pm in Mendocino) [Official, USNO].
  - CA-1, CA-128 and CA-20 are **unlit two-lane roads with frequent tight curves**. Plan arrivals before dark, and don't do a "quick sunset drive" back along CA-1 cliffs.
- **Deer:** deer are common along these rural roads, especially at dusk and dawn. Slow down where you see deer-crossing signs, use high beams when no one is coming, and **brake, don't swerve**: swerving on a cliff road is worse than hitting the deer. (General advice; local collision statistics **UNVERIFIED**.)
- **Turnouts (CA law):** "You **must** use a turnout area or lane to let other vehicles pass when you are driving slowly on a two-lane road, where passing is unsafe, and there are **five or more vehicles following you**." [Official, [CA DMV handbook](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/california-driver-handbook/navigating-the-roads/)]. Locals drive these roads fast. Let them by.
- **Fog:** "Drive slowly. Use your **low-beam** headlights. High-beam headlights will reflect back and cause glare." [Official, [CA DMV](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/california-driver-handbook/safe-driving/)]
- **Rain:**
  - The first storm after a dry spell makes roads slick.
  - Watch for **rocks and mud on CA-1** after rain (there's an active slide site now, §1.7) and for **standing water on CA-128 near the Navarro** (§8).
  - "**Avoid flooded roads**: vehicles can become trapped and saltwater damages engines" [Official, [Coastal Commission king-tide safety](https://www.coastal.ca.gov/kingtides/safety.html)].
- **Cold mornings inland:** normal lows are ~34 °F in Willits and ~38 °F in Ukiah (§9.1), so **shaded curves on CA-20/CA-128 can be icy early in the morning** after a clear night. Start the Saturday return after ~8:30 am.
- **Alcohol:**
  - BAC limit "0.08% … over 21", **"0.01% … under 21"**. An open container "must [be kept] in the trunk or place where passengers do not sit" [Official, [CA DMV](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/california-driver-handbook/alcohol-and-drugs/)].
  - Anderson Valley wine and beer tasting means **the driver doesn't taste**. Rotate drivers by day.
- **Motion sickness** (CA-1 north of Jenner, the Yorkville grade on CA-128, CA-20) [Official, [CDC Yellow Book](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/travel-air-sea/motion-sickness.html)]:
  - Look at the horizon, **don't read or scroll**, and keep breathing steady. Prone people sit in front.
  - OTC **dimenhydrinate (Dramamine)** or **meclizine (Bonine, "Dramamine Less Drowsy")** works best taken "**1–2 hours before travel**". "The most common side effect is **drowsiness**," so **the driver must not take it**.
- **Driver fatigue:** Route B is a 6–7 h day with stops. Switch drivers at Gualala or Point Arena.
- **Phones:** set navigation **before** you start. Weak signal (§12) means rerouting may fail mid-curve.

## 10. Ocean safety (sneaker waves, cliffs, logs)

- **NWS Eureka, Surf Zone Forecast (11/10):** "CAUTION: **Most Northwest California beaches are always dangerous due to cold water and consistent wave action** … the general public should **simply stay out of the water** along the north coast of California." [Official, [NWS EKA SRF](https://forecast.weather.gov/product.php?site=eka&issuedby=EKA&product=SRF)]
- **CA State Parks, Sonoma Coast:** "Strong rip currents, heavy surf and **sudden ground swells** make surf play dangerous." "**Never turn your back to the ocean.** Always stay well above the highest water marks in the sand." [Official, [Parks](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=451)]
- **Coastal Commission, King Tide safety** [Official, [coastal.ca.gov](https://www.coastal.ca.gov/kingtides/safety.html)]:
  - "Standing on **rocks, seawalls, or bluff edges** can be particularly dangerous as powerful waves may emerge without warning."
  - "Stay alert **even at low tide**."
  - "Avoid climbing on **unstable logs** and rocks."
  - "Wear closed-toed shoes with good traction."
  - "**no photograph is worth risking your life**."
- **Practical rules for this group (my synthesis):**
  1. Look at the water for a few minutes before you walk down. Big sets can arrive after several quiet minutes.
  2. Stay off driftwood logs on the beach, since a wave can roll them. Stay off wet rocks and the edges of bluff-top trails, which can be undercut.
  3. Keep phones and cameras on a strap, and **don't chase a dropped item** toward the water.
  4. **If someone is swept in, don't go in after them.** Call **911**, keep them in sight, and throw something that floats.
  5. On the Mendocino Headlands, stay on the trails. The cliffs are sheer and crumbly.
- **Wave conditions:** check the NWS EKA Surf Zone Forecast (above) and the "GFS-Wave **Sneaker** and Surf Calculations" (https://www.weather.gov/eka/gfsSneakerIndex) the day before. Watch for any **Beach Hazards Statement** or **High Surf Advisory** in the NWS alerts. Snapshot: buoy 46014 (Point Arena) showed **3.5 m (≈11.5 ft) waves at 14 s** on 11/11, which is a long-period swell [Official, [NDBC 46014](https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/station_page.php?station=46014)].
- **Tsunami:** if you feel strong shaking on the coast, **move inland or uphill right away**, without waiting for an official warning. Tsunami hazard maps are at https://www.conservation.ca.gov/cgs/tsunami/maps [Official, CGS]. Earthquake basics are at [earthquakecountry.org/step5](https://www.earthquakecountry.org/step5/).

## 14. Packing list (late-November North Coast plus a road trip)

**Clothing (layers; 40s–50s °F, windy, ~50 % chance of rain each day)**
- Waterproof rain shell with a hood, a warm mid-layer (fleece or down), a beanie and light gloves for the windy headlands at sunset
- **Shoes with real grip that can get wet and muddy** (closed-toe; trails and headlands are slick). A spare pair of socks.
- Something nicer for the Thanksgiving dinner, if you've booked one

**Car**
- Phone mounts, **12V/USB chargers**, a **battery bank per person**
- **Headlamp or flashlight** (dark by 5:20 pm; power outages)
- Snacks and water. Thanksgiving Day closures are likely; holiday store hours are **UNVERIFIED**, so buy breakfast items for Friday morning in advance.
- **Motion-sickness medicine for passengers**, ginger candy, and a plastic bag
- Small first-aid kit, any personal medications, hand warmers
- Microfiber towel (wet seats, foggy windows), trash bags
- **A few $10 and $20 bills** (park self-pay, card readers down in an outage)
- Paper backup: insurance card, registration, lodging confirmation, a printed or offline map
- Optional: binoculars (seals, sea lions, maybe early gray whales). Thermos.

**Documents**
- Driver's licenses (each driver), ID or passport for tastings, health-insurance card, credit card
- FasTrak short-term plate account set up, or accept the invoice (§4)

**Not needed:** tire chains (no chain areas on the planned routes in normal weather; check if a cold storm is forecast, §1.4), swimwear.

## 15. 1–2 days before (Tue 11/24 – Wed 11/25) checklist

   - whether the **11/13 utility signals** are gone
   - any **Navarro / CA-128** flooding

Check for **Beach Hazards Statement / High Surf Advisory / Flood Watch / Wind Advisory**, and the **CPC week 3–4 (11/20 update)**. If a big atmospheric river is forecast for Thu, **switch to Route A or C, or rethink the trip**.
3. **Navarro gauge** (https://water.noaa.gov/gauges/nvrc1): forecast staying below **17 ft**? (≥ 23 ft = CA-128 closed.)
4. **AAA 2026 Thanksgiving forecast** (newsroom.aaa.com), to confirm the 2025 traffic windows still hold.
5. **Tides and sunset:** re-check NOAA (§9.4) and plan any tidepool or Bowling Ball Beach stop for **Thu 3:30 pm – sunset** only.
6. **Lodging:** confirm the check-in window (small inns may close the front desk early on Thanksgiving), parking (on-site or street), and the Wi-Fi and power situation. Check the PG&E outage map if a storm is coming.

10. **Phones:** download **offline maps** for both routes. Save the lodging address and the ER address. Share the itinerary with someone not on the trip. Install **MendoAlert/Nixle** (text 95460 to 888777) if you want local alerts.
11. **Agree on drivers and order:** decide who drives which leg, who navigates, and who is the non-drinking driver on the Anderson Valley leg. Decide the latest acceptable time to be off CA-1 curves (≈ 4:45 pm).
